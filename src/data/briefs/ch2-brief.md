### 1: Experiencing the Power Play
eyebrow: Exponential Growth

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- Multiplicative growth (also called exponential growth) is growth where a quantity doubles (or multiplies by a fixed factor) at each step, as opposed to additive growth.
- After each fold of a paper, the thickness doubles.
- After 10 folds, the thickness increases by 1024 times (2 multiplied by itself 10 times).
- The pattern of doubling shows that from any starting point, after 10 folds the thickness multiplies by 1024.

**Worked examples (1 sentence each, only if source walks through solution):**
- The thickness after 10 folds is 1.024 cm, after 17 folds about 131 cm, after 26 folds about 670 m, after 30 folds about 10.7 km, and after 46 folds more than 7,00,000 km.

**Callout cards (1-3 per section):**
- title: "Exponential Growth" | content: "When a quantity doubles (or multiplies by a fixed factor) at each step, it is called multiplicative or exponential growth. This growth is much faster than additive growth."

**Widget idea:** An interactive paper-folding simulation where the user clicks to fold and sees the thickness grow exponentially, with a progress bar comparing to real-world heights (Burj Khalifa, plane altitude, Moon distance).

**Key terms:** exponential growth, multiplicative growth, doubling

### 2: Exponential Notation and Operations
eyebrow: Powers and Exponents

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- n squared (n²) means n × n.
- n cubed (n³) means n × n × n.
- n raised to the power a (n^a) means n multiplied by itself a times.
- In the expression n^a, n is the base and a is the exponent (or power).
- The product rule: n^a × n^b = n^(a+b), where a and b are counting numbers.
- The power of a power rule: (n^a)^b = n^(a×b), where a and b are counting numbers.
- The product of powers with same exponent: m^a × n^a = (m × n)^a, where a is a counting number.
- The quotient of powers with same exponent: m^a ÷ n^a = (m ÷ n)^a, where a is a counting number.
- A number can be expressed as a product of its prime factors in exponential form.
- (–1) raised to an odd power is –1; (–1) raised to an even power is 1.
- 0 raised to any positive power is 0.

**Worked examples (1 sentence each, only if source walks through solution):**
- Expressing 32400 as a product of prime factors in exponential form: 32400 = 2^4 × 5^2 × 3^4.
- Writing p^4 × p^6 in exponential form: p^4 × p^6 = p^10.
- Computing 4^6 by grouping as (4^3)^2 = 64^2 = 4096 and also as (4^2)^3 = 16^3 = 4096.
- The Magical Pond problem: if lotuses double daily and the pond is full on day 30, it is half full on day 29.
- The Magical Pond problem with tripling: after 4 days in the doubling pond there are 2^4 lotuses; after 4 more days in the tripling pond there are 2^4 × 3^4 = (2×3)^4 = 6^4 lotuses.
- The Combinations problem: with 4 dresses and 3 caps, there are 4 × 3 = 12 combinations.
- The Combinations problem: with 7 dresses, 2 hats, and 3 pairs of shoes, there are 7 × 2 × 3 = 42 combinations.
- The password problem: a 2-digit lock has 10 × 10 = 100 combinations; a 3-digit lock has 100 × 10 = 1000 combinations; a 5-digit lock has 10^5 = 1,00,000 combinations.

**Callout cards (1-3 per section):**
- title: "Exponential Notation" | content: "n^a means n multiplied by itself a times. n is the base, a is the exponent. For example, 5^4 = 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 = 625."
- title: "Product Rule" | content: "n^a × n^b = n^(a+b). When multiplying powers with the same base, add the exponents."
- title: "Power of a Power Rule" | content: "(n^a)^b = n^(a×b). When raising a power to another power, multiply the exponents."

**Widget idea:** An interactive exponent calculator where users input a base and exponent, and see the expanded multiplication and the result. Also a drag-and-drop activity to match exponential expressions with their expanded forms.

**Key terms:** base, exponent, power, exponential form, product rule, power of a power rule

### 3: The Other Side of Powers
eyebrow: Negative and Zero Exponents

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- The quotient rule: n^a ÷ n^b = n^(a-b), where n ≠ 0 and a and b are counting numbers and a > b.
- n^0 = 1, where n ≠ 0.
- n^(-a) = 1 / n^a, where n ≠ 0.
- 1 / n^(-a) = n^a.
- The general forms (product rule, power of a power rule, quotient rule) hold true for integer exponents (including negative and zero).

**Worked examples (1 sentence each, only if source walks through solution):**
- Halving a line of 16 units (2^4) once gives 2^3 = 8 units; halving twice gives 2^2 = 4 units; halving three times gives 2^1 = 2 units, showing 2^4 ÷ 2^3 = 2^(4-3) = 2^1.
- Defining 2^0: 2^0 = 2^(4-4) = 2^4 ÷ 2^4 = 1.
- Defining 2^(-1): 2^4 ÷ 2^5 = 2^(4-5) = 2^(-1) = 1/2.
- Defining 2^(-6): 2^4 ÷ 2^10 = 2^(4-10) = 2^(-6) = 1/64.

**Callout cards (1-3 per section):**
- title: "Zero Exponent" | content: "Any non-zero number raised to the power 0 equals 1. n^0 = 1, where n ≠ 0."
- title: "Negative Exponent" | content: "n^(-a) = 1 / n^a. A negative exponent means the reciprocal of the positive power."

**Widget idea:** An interactive number line (Power Lines) where users can slide to see powers of a base (e.g., 4 or 7) and how they relate to each other, including negative exponents.

**Key terms:** quotient rule, zero exponent, negative exponent

### 4: Powers of 10
eyebrow: Scientific Notation

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- Any number can be written in expanded form using powers of 10.
- Decimal numbers can be written using powers of 10, including negative powers for decimal places.
- Scientific notation (standard form) writes a number as x × 10^y, where x ≥ 1 and x < 10 (the coefficient), and y is an integer (the exponent).
- The exponent in scientific notation indicates the number of digits in the number.
- The number of digits in the coefficient reflects how precisely the number is known.
- Large numbers are often rounded-off estimates, and scientific notation makes them easier to read and compare.

**Worked examples (1 sentence each, only if source walks through solution):**
- Writing 47561 in expanded form using powers of 10: (4 × 10^4) + (7 × 10^3) + (5 × 10^2) + (6 × 10^1) + (1 × 10^0).
- Writing 561.903 using powers of 10: (5 × 10^2) + (6 × 10^1) + (1 × 10^0) + (9 × 10^(-1)) + (0 × 10^(-2)) + (3 × 10^(-3)).
- Expressing 5900 in scientific notation: 5.9 × 10^3.
- Expressing 20800 in scientific notation: 2.08 × 10^4.
- Expressing 80,00,000 in scientific notation: 8 × 10^6.

**Callout cards (1-3 per section):**
- title: "Scientific Notation" | content: "A number in scientific notation is written as x × 10^y, where 1 ≤ x < 10 and y is an integer. For example, 5900 = 5.9 × 10^3."

**Widget idea:** An interactive converter where users input a large number and see it converted to scientific notation, and vice versa.

**Key terms:** scientific notation, standard form, coefficient, exponent, powers of 10

### 5: Did You Ever Wonder?
eyebrow: Estimation and Large Numbers

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- To solve real-world estimation problems, follow these steps: 1) Make an instinctive guess. 2) Describe the relationships among the quantities needed. 3) Make reasonable assumptions and approximations. 4) Compute and find the answer.
- Linear growth is additive (e.g., adding a fixed amount each step).
- Exponential growth is multiplicative (e.g., doubling each step).
- Large numbers can be represented and compared using powers of 10.
- The Indian number system uses: lakh (10^5), crore (10^7), arab (10^9), kharab (10^11), neel (10^13), padma (10^15), shankh (10^17), maha shankh (10^19).
- The International number system uses: million (10^6), billion (10^9), trillion (10^12), quadrillion (10^15), quintillion (10^18), sextillion (10^21), septillion (10^24), etc.
- A googol is 10^100. A googolplex is 10^(googol).

**Worked examples (1 sentence each, only if source walks through solution):**
- The Tulābhāra problem: assuming Roxie weighs 45 kg and jaggery costs ₹70/kg, the worth is 45 × 70 = ₹3150; assuming Estu weighs 50 kg and wheat costs ₹50/kg, the worth is 50 × 50 = ₹2500.
- The ladder to the Moon problem: with 20 cm per step, the number of steps to cover 3,84,400 km is 1,92,20,00,000 steps (1.922 × 10^9 steps), showing linear growth.

**Callout cards (1-3 per section):**
- title: "Linear vs. Exponential Growth" | content: "Linear growth adds a fixed amount each step (e.g., 20 + 20 + 20...). Exponential growth multiplies by a fixed factor each step (e.g., 0.001 × 2 × 2 × 2...). Exponential growth is much faster."

**Widget idea:** An interactive comparison tool where users can see linear growth (adding a fixed number) vs. exponential growth (doubling) side-by-side on a graph.

**Key terms:** estimation, approximation, linear growth, exponential growth, lakh, crore, arab, kharab, neel, padma, shankh, million, billion, trillion, googol
