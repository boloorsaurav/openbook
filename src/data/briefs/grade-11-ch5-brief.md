### 2: Inequalities
eyebrow: Section 5.2

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- Two real numbers or two algebraic expressions related by the symbols less than, greater than, less than or equal to, or greater than or equal to form an inequality.
- Numerical inequalities involve only numbers, e.g., 3 less than 5.
- Literal inequalities involve variables, e.g., x less than 5.
- Double inequalities combine two inequalities, e.g., 3 less than x less than 5.
- Strict inequalities use only less than or greater than.
- Slack inequalities use less than or equal to or greater than or equal to.
- Linear inequalities in one variable have the form ax + b less than 0, ax + b greater than 0, ax + b less than or equal to 0, or ax + b greater than or equal to 0, where a is not equal to 0.
- Linear inequalities in two variables have the form ax + by less than c, ax + by greater than c, ax + by less than or equal to c, or ax + by greater than or equal to c, where a and b are not equal to 0.
- Quadratic inequalities in one variable have the form ax squared + bx + c less than or equal to 0 or ax squared + bx + c greater than 0, where a is not equal to 0.
- This chapter only studies linear inequalities in one and two variables.

### 3: Algebraic Solutions of Linear Inequalities in One Variable and their Graphical Representation
eyebrow: Section 5.3

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- A solution of an inequality in one variable is a value of the variable that makes the inequality a true statement.
- The set of all solutions is called the solution set.
- Solving inequalities by trial and error is inefficient; systematic rules are needed.
- Rule 1: Equal numbers may be added to or subtracted from both sides of an inequality without affecting the sign of inequality.
- Rule 2: Both sides of an inequality can be multiplied or divided by the same positive number without affecting the sign of inequality.
- Rule 2 (special case): When both sides are multiplied or divided by a negative number, the sign of inequality is reversed (less than becomes greater than, less than or equal to becomes greater than or equal to, and so on).
- Unless stated otherwise, inequalities are solved in the set of real numbers.
- The solution set for x less than a is written as x in the interval from negative infinity to a.
- The solution set for x greater than a is written as x in the interval from a to infinity.
- The solution set for x less than or equal to a is written as x in the interval from negative infinity to a, including a.
- The solution set for x greater than or equal to a is written as x in the interval from a to infinity, including a.
- The solution set for x less than a is represented on a number line by putting a circle on the number a and darkening the line to the left of a.
- The solution set for x greater than a is represented on a number line by putting a circle on the number a and darkening the line to the right of a.
- The solution set for x less than or equal to a is represented on a number line by putting a dark circle on the number a and darkening the line to the left of a.
- The solution set for x greater than or equal to a is represented on a number line by putting a dark circle on the number a and darkening the line to the right of a.
- To solve a system of inequalities, find the values of the variable that satisfy all inequalities simultaneously.
- To solve a double inequality, solve both inequalities simultaneously.
