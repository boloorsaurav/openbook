### 1: Introduction
eyebrow: Introduction

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- Coordinate geometry combines algebra and geometry to study geometric figures.
- The location of a point in the XY-plane is given by its distances from the y-axis (x-coordinate) and the x-axis (y-coordinate).
- The distance between two points P(x1, y1) and Q(x2, y2) is given by the formula: square root of [(x2 - x1) squared + (y2 - y1) squared].
- The coordinates of a point dividing the line segment joining (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) internally in the ratio m:n are given by: x = (m*x2 + n*x1) / (m + n), y = (m*y2 + n*y1) / (m + n).
- The coordinates of the mid-point of the line segment joining (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) are: x = (x1 + x2)/2, y = (y1 + y2)/2.
- The area of a triangle with vertices (x1, y1), (x2, y2), and (x3, y3) is: one-half times the absolute value of [x1(y2 - y3) + x2(y3 - y1) + x3(y1 - y2)].
- If the area of triangle ABC is zero, then the three points A, B, and C are collinear (lie on a line).

**Worked examples (brief, 1 sentence each — only if the source walks through a solution):**

- Found the point dividing the segment joining A(1, -3) and B(-3, 9) internally in the ratio 1:3 to be (0, 0).
- Calculated the area of the triangle with vertices (4, 4), (3, -2), and (-3, 16) to be 27 square units.

### 2: Slope of a Line
eyebrow: Section 9.2

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- The inclination of a line is the angle (theta) made by the line with the positive direction of the x-axis, measured anti-clockwise. The range of inclination is 0 degrees to 180 degrees.
- Lines parallel to or coinciding with the x-axis have an inclination of 0 degrees.
- The inclination of a vertical line (parallel to or coinciding with the y-axis) is 90 degrees.
- The slope or gradient of a line is defined as tan(theta), where theta is the inclination of the line.
- The slope of a line with an inclination of 90 degrees is not defined.
- The slope of a line is denoted by m.
- The slope of the x-axis is zero, and the slope of the y-axis is not defined.
- The slope of a non-vertical line passing through two points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) is given by m = (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1).
- Two non-vertical lines are parallel if and only if their slopes are equal.
- Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular to each other if and only if the product of their slopes is -1 (their slopes are negative reciprocals of each other).
- The acute angle (theta) between two non-vertical lines with slopes m1 and m2 is given by: tan(theta) = |(m2 - m1) / (1 + m1*m2)|, provided 1 + m1*m2 is not equal to 0.
- The obtuse angle (phi) between two lines can be found using phi = 180 degrees - theta.

**Worked examples (brief, 1 sentence each — only if the source walks through a solution):**
- Example 1: Found the slope for lines passing through given points and for a line with a 60-degree inclination.
- Example 2: Found the slope of the other line given the angle between two lines is 45 degrees and one slope is 1/2, resulting in two possible slopes: 3 or -1/3.
- Example 3: Found the value of x such that the line through (-2, 6) and (4, 8) is perpendicular to the line through (8, 12) and (x, 24), resulting in x = 4.

**Callout cards (1–3 per section — pick genuinely important insights):**
- title: "Parallel Lines Condition" | content: "Two non-vertical lines are parallel if and only if their slopes are equal (m1 = m2)."
- title: "Perpendicular Lines Condition" | content: "Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular if and only if the product of their slopes is -1 (m1 * m2 = -1)."
- title: "Angle Between Two Lines" | content: "The acute angle theta between two lines with slopes m1 and m2 is given by tan(theta) = |(m2 - m1) / (1 + m1*m2)|."

**Widget idea:** An interactive graph where users can drag two lines and see their slopes, the angle between them, and a label indicating if they are parallel or perpendicular.

**Key terms:** inclination, slope, gradient, parallel lines, perpendicular lines, angle between two lines

### 3: Various Forms of the Equation of a Line
eyebrow: Section 9.3

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- The equation of a horizontal line at a distance 'a' from the x-axis is either y = a or y = -a.
- The equation of a vertical line at a distance 'b' from the y-axis is either x = b or x = -b.
- The point-slope form of a line: The equation of a line with slope 'm' passing through a fixed point (x0, y0) is y - y0 = m(x - x0).
- The two-point form of a line: The equation of a line passing through two points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) is y - y1 = [(y2 - y1)/(x2 - x1)] * (x - x1).
- The slope-intercept form of a line: The equation of a line with slope 'm' and y-intercept 'c' is y = mx + c.
- The equation of a line with slope 'm' and x-intercept 'd' is y = m(x - d).
- The intercept form of a line: The equation of a line making x-intercept 'a' and y-intercept 'b' is x/a + y/b = 1.
- Any equation of the form Ax + By + C = 0, where A and B are not both zero, is called the general linear equation or general equation of a line.

**Worked examples (brief, 1 sentence each — only if the source walks through a solution):**
- Example 4: Found the equations of lines parallel to the axes passing through (-2, 3) to be y = 3 and x = -2.
- Example 5: Found the equation of the line through (-2, 3) with slope -4 to be 4x + y + 5 = 0.
- Example 6: Found the equation of the line through (1, -1) and (3, 5) to be -3x + y + 4 = 0.
- Example 7: Wrote the equation of a line with slope 1/2 and y-intercept -3/2 as 2y - x + 3 = 0, and with x-intercept 4 as 2y - x + 4 = 0.
- Example 8: Found the equation of the line with intercepts -3 and 2 to be 2x - 3y + 6 = 0.

**Callout cards (1–3 per section — pick genuinely important insights):**
- title: "Point-Slope Form" | content: "The equation of a line with slope m through a point (x0, y0) is y - y0 = m(x - x0)."
- title: "Slope-Intercept Form" | content: "The equation of a line with slope m and y-intercept c is y = mx + c."
- title: "Intercept Form" | content: "The equation of a line with x-intercept a and y-intercept b is x/a + y/b = 1."

**Widget idea:** An interactive tool where users can input different parameters (slope, point, intercepts) and see the corresponding equation of the line and its graph update.

**Key terms:** horizontal line, vertical line, point-slope form, two-point form, slope-intercept form, intercept form, general linear equation

### 4: Distance of a Point From a Line
eyebrow: Section 9.4

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- The distance of a point from a line is the length of the perpendicular drawn from the point to the line.
- The perpendicular distance (d) of a line Ax + By + C = 0 from a point (x1, y1) is given by: d = |Ax1 + By1 + C| / square root of (A squared + B squared).
- The distance (d) between two parallel lines y = mx + c1 and y = mx + c2 is given by: d = |c1 - c2| / square root of (1 + m squared).
- The distance (d) between two parallel lines Ax + By + C1 = 0 and Ax + By + C2 = 0 is given by: d = |C1 - C2| / square root of (A squared + B squared).

**Worked examples (brief, 1 sentence each — only if the source walks through a solution):**
- Example 9: Found the distance of point (3, -5) from line 3x - 4y - 26 = 0 to be 3/5 units.
- Example 10: Found the distance between parallel lines 3x - 4y + 7 = 0 and 3x - 4y + 5 = 0 to be 2/5 units.

**Callout cards (1–3 per section — pick genuinely important insights):**
- title: "Distance from a Point to a Line" | content: "The perpendicular distance from a point (x1, y1) to the line Ax + By + C = 0 is |Ax1 + By1 + C| / sqrt(A^2 + B^2)."
- title: "Distance Between Parallel Lines" | content: "The distance between two parallel lines Ax + By + C1 = 0 and Ax + By + C2 = 0 is |C1 - C2| / sqrt(A^2 + B^2)."

**Widget idea:** An interactive graph showing a line and a point. Users can drag the point and see the perpendicular distance to the line calculated and displayed.

**Key terms:** perpendicular distance, distance from a point to a line, distance between parallel lines

### 5: Miscellaneous Examples
eyebrow: Miscellaneous

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- Three lines are concurrent if they all pass through a single common point.
- To find the distance of a line from a point measured along another line, find the point of intersection of the two lines and then calculate the distance between the given point and the intersection point.
- The image of a point in a line can be found by using the fact that the line is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining the point and its image.
- The area of a triangle formed by the lines y = m1x + c1, y = m2x + c2, and x = 0 is: (1/2) * |(c1 - c2) squared / (m1 - m2)|.
- The path of a point that is equidistant from two given lines is a straight line (the angle bisector of the lines).

**Worked examples (brief, 1 sentence each — only if the source walks through a solution):**
- Example 11: Found the value of k for which three given lines are concurrent to be k = -2.
- Example 12: Found the distance of line 4x - y = 0 from point P(4, 1) measured along a line making a 135-degree angle to be 3*sqrt(2) units.
- Example 13: Found the image of point (1, 2) in the line x - 3y + 4 = 0 to be (6/5, 7/5).
- Example 14: Derived the formula for the area of a triangle formed by two lines and the y-axis.
- Example 15: Found the equation of a line whose segment between two given lines is bisected at (1, 5) to be 107x - 3y - 92 = 0.
- Example 16: Showed that the path of a point equidistant from lines 3x - 2y = 5 and 3x + 2y = 5 is a straight line (y = 0 or x = 5/3).

**Callout cards (1–3 per section — pick genuinely important insights):**
- title: "Concurrent Lines" | content: "Three lines are concurrent if the point of intersection of any two of them lies on the third line."
- title: "Image of a Point in a Line" | content: "The image of a point in a line can be found by using the line as the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining the point and its image."
