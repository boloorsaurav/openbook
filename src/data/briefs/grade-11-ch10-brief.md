## Sections

### 1: Introduction
eyebrow: Introduction

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- Conic sections (conics) are curves obtained as intersections of a plane with a double-napped right circular cone.
- The curves studied are circles, ellipses, parabolas, and hyperbolas.
- These curves have applications in planetary motion, telescope and antenna design, and reflectors in flashlights and automobile headlights.

### 2: Sections of a Cone
eyebrow: Section 10.2

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- A double-napped right circular hollow cone is generated by rotating a line m around a fixed vertical line l, keeping the angle alpha between them constant.
- The point V where the lines intersect is called the vertex.
- The fixed vertical line l is the axis of the cone.
- The rotating line m is called a generator of the cone.
- The vertex separates the cone into two parts called nappes.
- The intersection of a plane with a cone is called a conic section.
- Let beta be the angle made by the intersecting plane with the vertical axis of the cone.
- When the plane cuts the nappe (other than the vertex): if beta equals 90 degrees, the section is a circle. If alpha is less than beta and beta is less than 90 degrees, the section is an ellipse. If beta equals alpha, the section is a parabola. If 0 is less than or equal to beta and beta is less than alpha, the plane cuts through both nappes and the curve is a hyperbola.
- When the plane cuts at the vertex: if alpha is less than beta and beta is less than or equal to 90 degrees, the section is a point. If beta equals alpha, the plane contains a generator and the section is a straight line (degenerated case of a parabola). If 0 is less than or equal to beta and beta is less than alpha, the section is a pair of intersecting straight lines (degenerated case of a hyperbola).

### 3: Circle
eyebrow: Section 10.3

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- A circle is the set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a fixed point in the plane.
- The fixed point is called the centre of the circle.
- The distance from the centre to a point on the circle is called the radius.
- The equation of a circle with centre at (h, k) and radius r is (x minus h) squared plus (y minus k) squared equals r squared.
- If the centre is at the origin (0,0), the equation simplifies to x squared plus y squared equals r squared.

**Worked examples (brief, 1 sentence each — only if the source walks through a solution):**

- Example 3: Found the centre (-4, -5) and radius 7 of the circle given by x squared plus y squared plus 8x plus 10y minus 8 equals 0 by completing the square.
- Example 4: Found the equation of a circle passing through (2, -2) and (3, 4) with its centre on the line x plus y equals 2, resulting in (x minus 0.7) squared plus (y minus 1.3) squared equals 12.58.

### 4: Parabola
eyebrow: Section 10.4

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- A parabola is the set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a fixed line (directrix) and a fixed point (focus) not on the line.
- If the fixed point lies on the fixed line, the set of points is a straight line through the fixed point and perpendicular to the fixed line (degenerate case of a parabola).
- A line through the focus and perpendicular to the directrix is called the axis of the parabola.
- The point of intersection of the parabola with its axis is called the vertex.
- The standard equation of a parabola with vertex at the origin and focus at (a, 0) where a is greater than 0, and directrix x equals negative a, is y squared equals 4ax.
- The standard equation of a parabola with vertex at the origin and focus at (negative a, 0) and directrix x equals a is y squared equals negative 4ax.
- The standard equation of a parabola with vertex at the origin and focus at (0, a) and directrix y equals negative a is x squared equals 4ay.
- The standard equation of a parabola with vertex at the origin and focus at (0, negative a) and directrix y equals a is x squared equals negative 4ay.
- If the equation has a y squared term, the axis of symmetry is along the x-axis. If the equation has an x squared term, the axis of symmetry is along the y-axis.
- When the axis of symmetry is along the x-axis, the parabola opens to the right if the coefficient of x is positive, and to the left if the coefficient of x is negative.
- When the axis of symmetry is along the y-axis, the parabola opens upwards if the coefficient of y is positive, and downwards if the coefficient of y is negative.
- The latus rectum of a parabola is a line segment perpendicular to the axis of the parabola, through the focus, whose endpoints lie on the parabola.
- The length of the latus rectum of the parabola y squared equals 4ax is 4a.

**Worked examples (brief, 1 sentence each — only if the source walks through a solution):**
- Example 5: Found the focus (2,0), directrix x equals negative 2, and latus rectum length 8 for the parabola y squared equals 8x.
- Example 6: Found the equation y squared equals 8x for a parabola with focus (2,0) and directrix x equals negative 2.
- Example 7: Found the equation x squared equals 8y for a parabola with vertex at (0,0) and focus at (0,2).
- Example 8: Found the equation 3x squared equals negative 4y for a parabola symmetric about the y-axis, passing through (2, negative 3).

**Callout cards (1–3 per section — pick genuinely important insights):**
- title: "Definition of a Parabola" | content: "A parabola is the set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a fixed line (directrix) and a fixed point (focus)."
- title: "Standard Equations of a Parabola" | content: "The four standard forms are y squared equals plus or minus 4ax and x squared equals plus or minus 4ay, depending on the orientation."
- title: "Latus Rectum of a Parabola" | content: "The latus rectum is a line segment through the focus perpendicular to the axis, with length 4a."

**Key terms:** parabola, directrix, focus, axis, vertex, latus rectum

### 5: Ellipse
eyebrow: Section 10.5

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- An ellipse is the set of all points in a plane, the sum of whose distances from two fixed points (foci) is a constant.
- The constant sum of distances is always greater than the distance between the two foci.
- The midpoint of the line segment joining the foci is called the centre of the ellipse.
- The line segment through the foci is called the major axis.
- The line segment through the centre and perpendicular to the major axis is called the minor axis.
- The endpoints of the major axis are called the vertices of the ellipse.
- The length of the major axis is 2a, the length of the minor axis is 2b, and the distance between the foci is 2c.
- The relationship between a, b, and c is: a squared equals b squared plus c squared, so c equals the square root of (a squared minus b squared).
- The eccentricity e of an ellipse is the ratio c over a. The foci are at a distance of ae from the centre.
- The standard equation of an ellipse with centre at the origin and major axis along the x-axis is x squared over a squared plus y squared over b squared equals 1.
- The standard equation of an ellipse with centre at the origin and major axis along the y-axis is x squared over b squared plus y squared over a squared equals 1.
- The ellipse is symmetric with respect to both coordinate axes.
- The foci always lie on the major axis. The major axis is along the x-axis if the denominator of x squared is larger, and along the y-axis if the denominator of y squared is larger.
- The latus rectum of an ellipse is a line segment perpendicular to the major axis through any of the foci, with endpoints on the ellipse.
- The length of the latus rectum of an ellipse is 2b squared over a.

**Worked examples (brief, 1 sentence each — only if the source walks through a solution):**
- Example 9: Found foci (plus or minus 4, 0), vertices (plus or minus 5, 0), major axis length 10, minor axis length 6, eccentricity 4/5, and latus rectum 18/5 for the ellipse x squared over 25 plus y squared over 9 equals 1.
- Example 10: Found foci (0, plus or minus square root of 5), vertices (0, plus or minus 3), major axis length 6, minor axis length 4, and eccentricity square root of 5 over 3 for the ellipse 9x squared plus 4y squared equals 36.
- Example 11: Found the equation x squared over 169 plus y squared over 144 equals 1 for an ellipse with vertices (plus or minus 13, 0) and foci (plus or minus 5, 0).
- Example 12: Found the equation x squared over 75 plus y squared over 100 equals 1 for an ellipse with major axis length 20 and foci (0, plus or minus 5).
- Example 13: Found the equation 7x squared plus 15y squared equals 247 for an ellipse with major axis along the x-axis, passing through (4, 3) and (negative 1, 4).

**Callout cards (1–3 per section — pick genuinely important insights):**
- title: "Definition of an Ellipse" | content: "An ellipse is the set of all points in a plane, the sum of whose distances from two fixed points (foci) is a constant."
- title: "Key Ellipse Parameters" | content: "The major axis length is 2a, the minor axis length is 2b, and the distance between foci is 2c, with the relationship a squared equals b squared plus c squared."
- title: "Eccentricity of an Ellipse" | content: "Eccentricity e equals c over a, and it measures how 'stretched' the ellipse is, with 0 being a circle and values approaching 1 being very elongated."

### 6: Hyperbola
eyebrow: Section 10.6

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- A hyperbola is the set of all points in a plane, the difference of whose distances from two fixed points (foci) is a constant.
- The term "difference" means the distance to the farther point minus the distance to the closer point.
- The midpoint of the line segment joining the foci is called the centre of the hyperbola.
- The line through the foci is called the transverse axis.
- The line through the centre and perpendicular to the transverse axis is called the conjugate axis.
- The points at which the hyperbola intersects the transverse axis are called the vertices.
- The distance between the two foci is 2c, the length of the transverse axis is 2a, and the length of the conjugate axis is 2b.
- The relationship between a, b, and c is: b squared equals c squared minus a squared.
- The eccentricity e of a hyperbola is the ratio c over a. Since c is greater than or equal to a, the eccentricity is never less than one. The foci are at a distance of ae from the centre.
- The standard equation of a hyperbola with centre at the origin and transverse axis along the x-axis is x squared over a squared minus y squared over b squared equals 1.
- The standard equation of a hyperbola with centre at the origin and transverse axis along the y-axis is y squared over a squared minus x squared over b squared equals 1.
- A hyperbola in which a equals b is called an equilateral hyperbola.
- The hyperbola is symmetric with respect to both coordinate axes.
- The foci are always on the transverse axis. The transverse axis is determined by the positive term: if the x squared term is positive, the transverse axis is along the x-axis; if the y squared term is positive, the transverse axis is along the y-axis.
- The latus rectum of a hyperbola is a line segment perpendicular to the transverse axis through any of the foci, with endpoints on the hyperbola.
- The length of the latus rectum of a hyperbola is 2b squared over a.

**Worked examples (brief, 1 sentence each — only if the source walks through a solution):**
- Example 14 (i): Found foci (plus or minus 5, 0), vertices (plus or minus 3, 0), eccentricity 5/3, and latus rectum 32/3 for the hyperbola x squared over 9 minus y squared over 16 equals 1.
- Example 14 (ii): Found foci (0, plus or minus square root of 17), vertices (0, plus or minus 4), eccentricity square root of 17 over 4, and latus rectum 1/2 for the hyperbola y squared minus 16x squared equals 16.
- Example 15: Found the equation 100 y squared minus 44 x squared equals 275 for a hyperbola with foci (0, plus or minus 3) and vertices (0, plus or minus square root of 11 over 2).
- Example 16: Found the equation 3y squared minus x squared equals 108 for a hyperbola with foci (0, plus or minus 12) and latus rectum length 36.

**Callout cards (1–3 per section — pick genuinely important insights):**
- title: "Definition of a Hyperbola" | content: "A hyperbola is the set of all points in a plane, the difference of whose distances from two fixed points (foci) is a constant."
- title: "Key Hyperbola Parameters" | content: "The transverse axis length is 2a, the conjugate axis length is 2b, and the distance between foci is 2c, with the relationship c squared equals a squared plus b squared."
- title: "Eccentricity of a Hyperbola" | content: "Eccentricity e equals c over a, and it is always greater than 1 for a hyperbola."

**Widget idea:** An interactive graphing tool where users can adjust the values of a and b to see how the hyperbola, its foci, and its vertices change on a coordinate plane.

**Key terms:** hyperbola, foci, centre, transverse axis, conjugate axis, vertices, eccentricity, latus rectum, equilateral hyperbola

### 7: Miscellaneous Examples
eyebrow: Applications

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- Parabolic mirrors have the property that all rays parallel to the axis are reflected through the focus.
- The shape of a uniformly loaded suspension bridge cable is a parabola.
- The locus of a point on a rod sliding between two coordinate axes can be an ellipse.

**Worked examples (brief, 1 sentence each — only if the source walks through a solution):**
- Example 17: Found the distance AB to be 60 cm for a parabolic mirror 45 cm deep with a focus 5 cm from the vertex.
- Example 18: Found the distance from the centre to a point of 1 cm deflection on a parabolic beam to be 2 times the square root of 6 metres.
- Example 19: Showed that the locus of a point P on a 15 cm rod sliding between axes, with AP equal to 6 cm, is the ellipse x squared over 81 plus y squared over 36 equals 1.

**Callout cards (1–3 per section — pick genuinely important insights):**
