### 1: Observing Similarity in Change
eyebrow: Similarity Through Scaling

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- Two images look similar when both their width and height change by the same factor (multiplication), not by the same difference (subtraction).
- When the width and height change by the same factor, the changes are proportional.
- When the width and height change by the same difference (subtraction), the images look distorted.

**Worked examples:**
- Comparing image A (60x40) with image C (30x20): width and height both halved, so images look similar.
- Comparing image A (60x40) with image B (40x20): height is half but width is not half, so images look different.

**Callout cards:**
- title: "Proportional Change" | content: "Two quantities change proportionally when both are multiplied by the same factor."

**Key terms:** proportional, factor, similarity, distortion

### 2: Ratios
eyebrow: Representing Proportional Relationships

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- A ratio a : b represents that for every 'a' units of the first quantity, there are 'b' units of the second quantity.
- The numbers a and b are called the terms of the ratio.
- Two ratios are proportional if their terms change by the same factor.
- A systematic way to check if ratios are proportional is to reduce them to their simplest form and see if they are the same.

**Worked examples:**
- Image A ratio 60:40, multiply both terms by 1/2 to get 30:20 (image C ratio), so they are proportional.
- Image A ratio 60:40, multiply both terms by 1.5 to get 90:60 (image D ratio), so they are proportional.

**Callout cards:**
- title: "Ratio Definition" | content: "A ratio a : b means for every 'a' units of the first quantity, there are 'b' units of the second quantity."

**Widget idea:** none

**Key terms:** ratio, terms of a ratio, proportional

### 3: Ratios in their Simplest Form
eyebrow: Reducing Ratios

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- Ratios can be reduced to their simplest form by dividing both terms by their HCF.
- When two ratios are the same in their simplest forms, they are said to be in proportion.
- The symbol '::' is used to indicate that two ratios are proportional (a : b :: c : d).

**Worked examples:**
- Image A ratio 60:40, HCF is 20, simplest form is 3:2.
- Image D ratio 90:60, HCF is 30, simplest form is 3:2. So 60:40 :: 90:60.
- Image B ratio 40:20, simplest form is 2:1.
- Image E ratio 60:60, simplest form is 1:1.

**Callout cards:**
- title: "Proportional Ratios" | content: "Two ratios are proportional if their simplest forms are the same. We write a : b :: c : d."

### 4: Problem Solving with Proportional Reasoning
eyebrow: Finding Unknowns in Proportions

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- To solve a proportion with an unknown term, find the factor by which the known term changed and apply the same factor to the other term.
- When the same number is added to or subtracted from both terms of a ratio, the ratio changes and is not proportional to the original ratio.
- Two ratios are proportional if their terms are equal when cross multiplied: if a : b :: c : d, then ad = bc.
- The cross multiplication rule can be used to find the fourth unknown: d = bc / a.
- In the Rule of Three (Trairasika), given pramāṇa (a), phala (b), and ichchhā (c), the ichchhāphala (d) = (phala × ichchhā) / pramāṇa.
- When solving proportion problems, all quantities must be in the same units.

**Worked examples:**
- Example 1: Check if 3:4 and 72:96 are proportional. HCF of 72 and 96 is 24, dividing gives 3:4. They are proportional.
- Example 2: 6 glasses need 10 spoons sugar. For 18 glasses, factor is 18/6 = 3. So 10 × 3 = 30 spoons. Answer: 30 spoons.
- Example 3: Nitin (60 ft, 3 bags) ratio 60:3 = 20:1. Hari (40 ft, 2 bags) ratio 40:2 = 20:1. Ratios are proportional, walls equally strong.
- Example 6: Neelima 3, mother 30, ratio 1:10. When Neelima is 12, mother is 39, ratio 12:39 = 4:13. Adding same number changes the ratio.
- Example 7: Find missing numbers proportional to 14:21. (a) ?:42, second term doubled, so first term = 14 × 2 = 28. (b) 6:?, factor = 6/14 = 3/7, so second term = 21 × 3/7 = 9. (c) 2:?, first term divided by 7, so second term = 21/7 = 3.
- Example 8: 120 students need 15 kg rice. For 80 students, factor = 80/120 = 2/3. Rice = 15 × 2/3 = 10 kg.
- Example 9: Car travels 90 km in 150 min. Distance in 4 hours (240 min). Proportion: 150:90 :: 240:x. Cross multiply: 150x = 240 × 90, x = 144 km.
- Example 10: Himachal tea 200g for ₹200, ratio 200:200 = 1:1. Meghalaya tea 1kg (1000g) for ₹800, ratio 1000:800 = 5:4. Not proportional. Cost of 1kg Himachal tea: 200g costs ₹200, so 1kg (5 × 200g) costs 5 × ₹200 = ₹1000. Himachal tea is more expensive.

**Callout cards:**
- title: "Cross Multiplication Rule" | content: "If a : b :: c : d, then ad = bc. The unknown term d = bc / a."
- title: "Rule of Three (Trairasika)" | content: "Given pramāṇa (a), phala (b), and ichchhā (c), the ichchhāphala (d) = (b × c) / a."

**Widget idea:** Interactive proportion solver: input three values, see the fourth calculated via cross multiplication with step-by-step animation.

### 5: Sharing, but Not Equally!
eyebrow: Dividing a Quantity in a Given Ratio

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- To divide a quantity x in the ratio m : n, first find the total number of groups: m + n.
- The size of each group is x / (m + n).
- The first part is m × [x / (m + n)] and the second part is n × [x / (m + n)].
- The resulting parts are in the ratio m : n.

**Worked examples:**
- Sharing 12 counters in ratio 3:1: total groups = 4, each group size = 12/4 = 3. First person gets 3 × 3 = 9, second gets 1 × 3 = 3.
- Sharing 42 counters in ratio 4:3: total groups = 7, each group size = 42/7 = 6. First person gets 4 × 6 = 24, second gets 3 × 6 = 18.
- Example 11: Prashanti invested ₹75,000, Bhuvan ₹25,000, ratio 3:1. Profit ₹4,000. Total groups = 4, each share = ₹1,000. Prashanti gets 3 × ₹1,000 = ₹3,000, Bhuvan gets ₹1,000.
- Example 12: 40 kg mixture, sand:cement = 3:1. Sand = (3/4) × 40 = 30 kg, cement = (1/4) × 40 = 10 kg. New ratio 5:2, sand remains 30 kg. Cement needed = (2/5) × 30 = 12 kg. Add 12 - 10 = 2 kg cement.

**Callout cards:**
- title: "Dividing in a Ratio" | content: "To divide x in ratio m:n, first part = m × x/(m+n), second part = n × x/(m+n)."

**Widget idea:** Interactive sharing simulator: drag sliders to set ratio and total quantity, see the parts animate into groups.

### 6: Unit Conversions
eyebrow: Converting Units for Proportional Problems

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- Solving proportion problems often requires converting units to be consistent.
- Length: 1 metre = 3.281 feet.
- Area: 1 square metre = 10.764 square feet; 1 acre = 43,560 square feet; 1 hectare = 10,000 square metres; 1 hectare = 2.471 acres.
- Volume: 1 millilitre (mL) = 1 cubic centimetre (cc); 1 litre = 1,000 mL or 1,000 cc.
- Temperature: Fahrenheit = (9/5) × Celsius + 32; Celsius = (5/9) × (Fahrenheit – 32).

**Worked examples:**
- 25°C to Fahrenheit: (9/5) × 25 + 32 = 45 + 32 = 77°F.

**Callout cards:**
- title: "Unit Conversion" | content: "Always convert quantities to the same units before setting up a proportion."

**Widget idea:** Interactive unit converter: select category (length, area, volume, temperature), input a value, see conversion in real time.

**Key terms:** unit conversion, metre, feet, acre, hectare, litre, millilitre, Celsius, Fahrenheit
