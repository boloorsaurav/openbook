### 1: Rectangles and Squares
eyebrow: Defining and deducing properties

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- A rectangle is a quadrilateral in which all angles are 90°.
- A rectangle can also be defined as a quadrilateral whose diagonals are equal and bisect each other.
- The diagonals of a rectangle are always equal in length.
- The diagonals of a rectangle always bisect each other (intersect at their midpoints).
- If the diagonals of a quadrilateral are equal and bisect each other, the quadrilateral is a rectangle, regardless of the angle between the diagonals.
- A square is a quadrilateral in which all angles are 90° and all sides are of equal length.
- Every square is a rectangle, but not every rectangle is a square.
- The diagonals of a square are equal, bisect each other, and intersect at 90° (right angles).
- The diagonals of a square bisect the angles of the square (each angle is divided into two 45° angles).
- A quadrilateral with all angles 90° must have opposite sides equal (proved by triangle congruence).
- A quadrilateral with all angles 90° must have opposite sides parallel (proved by transversal property: interior angles on same side sum to 180°).
- The sum of the interior angles of a triangle is 180°.
- In an isosceles triangle, angles opposite equal sides are equal.
- Vertically opposite angles are equal.
- Angles forming a linear pair sum to 180°.
- Congruence conditions: SAS, AAS, SSS, ASA.

**Worked examples (1 sentence each, only if source walks through solution):**
- Deduction 1: Proving diagonals of a rectangle are equal using SAS congruence of triangles ADC and DAB.
- Deduction 2: Proving diagonals of a rectangle bisect each other using AAS congruence of triangles AOB and COD.
- Deduction 3: Showing that if diagonals are equal and bisect each other, the quadrilateral is a rectangle, regardless of the angle between them, using isosceles triangle properties and angle sums.
- Deduction 4: Proving that a quadrilateral with all angles 90° must have opposite sides equal, using AAS congruence of triangles BAD and DCB.
- Deduction 5: Proving that the diagonals of a square intersect at 90° using SSS congruence of triangles BOA and BOC.
- Finding the angles formed by diagonals of a square: showing each diagonal divides the square's angles into two 45° angles using isosceles triangle properties.

- title: "Key Property of Diagonals" | content: "In a rectangle, diagonals are equal and bisect each other. In a square, diagonals are equal, bisect each other, and intersect at right angles, and they also bisect the angles of the square."

### 2: Angles in a Quadrilateral
eyebrow: Sum of interior angles

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- The sum of all interior angles in any quadrilateral is 360°.
- This is proved by drawing a diagonal, which divides the quadrilateral into two triangles, each with an angle sum of 180°.
- It is impossible for a quadrilateral to have three right angles and a fourth angle that is not a right angle, because the sum would not be 360°.

**Worked examples (1 sentence each, only if source walks through solution):**
- Proving the sum of angles in a quadrilateral is 360° by drawing diagonal SM in quadrilateral SOME and summing angles of triangles SEM and SOM.

**Callout cards (1-3 per section):**
- title: "Angle Sum of a Quadrilateral" | content: "The sum of the measures of the four interior angles of any quadrilateral is always 360°."

**Widget idea:** Interactive quadrilateral where user can drag vertices. The four interior angles are displayed and update in real-time, with a running total that always equals 360°.

**Key terms:** angle sum, diagonal, triangle

### 3: More Quadrilaterals with Parallel Opposite Sides
eyebrow: Parallelograms

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- A parallelogram is a quadrilateral in which opposite sides are parallel.
- A rectangle is a special parallelogram with all angles 90°.
- A square is a special rectangle and a special parallelogram.
- In a parallelogram, adjacent angles are supplementary (sum to 180°).
- In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal.
- The opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal in length.
- The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
- The diagonals of a parallelogram are not necessarily equal.
- The diagonals of a parallelogram do not necessarily intersect at a particular angle.

**Worked examples (1 sentence each, only if source walks through solution):**
- Deduction 6: Finding all angles of a parallelogram given one angle (30°) using the property that adjacent angles sum to 180° and opposite angles are equal.
- Deduction 7: Proving opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal using AAS congruence of triangles ABD and CDB.
- Deduction 8: Proving diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other using ASA congruence of triangles AOE and YOS.

**Callout cards (1-3 per section):**
- title: "Parallelogram Definition" | content: "A parallelogram is a quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel."
- title: "Parallelogram Properties" | content: "Opposite sides are equal and parallel. Opposite angles are equal. Adjacent angles sum to 180°. Diagonals bisect each other."

**Widget idea:** Interactive parallelogram where user can drag one vertex. All side lengths, angle measures, and diagonal intersection point update in real-time, demonstrating that opposite sides/angles remain equal and diagonals always bisect each other.

**Key terms:** parallelogram, adjacent angles, opposite angles, supplementary, transversal, alternate angles, AAS, ASA

### 4: Quadrilaterals with Equal Sidelengths
eyebrow: Rhombus

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- A rhombus is a quadrilateral in which all four sides have the same length.
- Every rhombus is a parallelogram (opposite sides are parallel).
- In a rhombus, opposite angles are equal.
- In a rhombus, adjacent angles sum to 180°.
- The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other.
- The diagonals of a rhombus intersect at right angles (90°).
- The diagonals of a rhombus bisect its interior angles.
- A square is a special rhombus with all angles 90°.
- A rhombus is not necessarily a square.

**Worked examples (1 sentence each, only if source walks through solution):**
- Deduction 9: Finding all angles of a rhombus given one angle (50°) using the property that the diagonal bisects the angles and forms isosceles triangles.
- Deduction 10: Proving diagonals of a rhombus intersect at 90° using congruence of triangles GEO and MEO.

**Callout cards (1-3 per section):**
- title: "Rhombus Definition" | content: "A rhombus is a quadrilateral with all four sides of equal length."
- title: "Rhombus Properties" | content: "All sides equal. Opposite sides parallel. Opposite angles equal. Adjacent angles sum to 180°. Diagonals bisect each other at right angles. Diagonals bisect the interior angles."

**Widget idea:** Interactive rhombus where user can drag one vertex. All side lengths remain equal, and the diagonals always intersect at 90° and bisect the angles.

**Key terms:** rhombus, isosceles triangle, diagonal, bisect, perpendicular, SSS

### 5: Playing with Quadrilaterals
eyebrow: Hands-on exploration

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- Joining two congruent triangles along a common side can form a quadrilateral.
- The type of quadrilateral formed depends on the type of triangle and how they are joined.
- Two congruent equilateral triangles joined along a common side form a rhombus.
- Two congruent isosceles triangles can form a kite or a parallelogram depending on how they are joined.
- Two congruent scalene triangles can form a parallelogram.

### 6: Kite and Trapezium
eyebrow: Other special quadrilaterals

**Concepts (exhaustive — every rule, definition, pattern):**
- A kite is a quadrilateral that can be labeled ABCD such that AB = BC and CD = DA (two distinct pairs of adjacent equal sides).
- In a kite, one diagonal (the one joining the vertices where equal sides meet) bisects the angles at those vertices.
- In a kite, one diagonal bisects the other diagonal and is perpendicular to it.
- A trapezium is a quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel opposite sides.
- In a trapezium, the angles on the same side of a transversal (the non-parallel side) sum to 180°.
- An isosceles trapezium is a trapezium where the non-parallel sides are equal in length.
- In an isosceles trapezium, the base angles (angles adjacent to each base) are equal.
- A parallelogram is a special trapezium (with two pairs of parallel sides).

**Worked examples (1 sentence each, only if source walks through solution):**
- Proving that in an isosceles trapezium, the angles opposite the equal sides are equal, by drawing perpendiculars to form a rectangle and using triangle congruence.

**Callout cards (1-3 per section):**
- title: "Kite Definition" | content: "A kite is a quadrilateral with two distinct pairs of adjacent sides that are equal in length."
- title: "Trapezium Definition" | content: "A trapezium is a quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides."
- title: "Isosceles Trapezium" | content: "An isosceles trapezium is a trapezium where the non-parallel sides are equal. Its base angles are equal."

**Widget idea:** Interactive kite and trapezium where user can drag vertices. For the kite, show that one diagonal is perpendicular to and bisects the other. For the trapezium, show that angles on the same side of a transversal sum to 180°.

**Key terms:** kite, trapezium, isosceles trapezium, base angles, transversal, perpendicular, bisect
