### 2: Complex Numbers
eyebrow: Section 4.2

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- The symbol i is defined as the square root of minus 1, so i squared equals minus 1.
- i is a solution of the equation x squared plus 1 equals 0.
- A complex number is a number of the form a plus ib, where a and b are real numbers.
- For a complex number z equals a plus ib, a is called the real part, denoted by Re z, and b is called the imaginary part, denoted by Im z.
- Two complex numbers z1 equals a plus ib and z2 equals c plus id are equal if and only if a equals c and b equals d.

**Worked examples:**
- Example 1: Solved 4x plus i times (3x minus y) equals 3 plus i times minus 6 to find x equals 3 over 4 and y equals 33 over 4, demonstrating equality of complex numbers.

### 3: Algebra of Complex Numbers
eyebrow: Section 4.3

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- Addition: For z1 equals a plus ib and z2 equals c plus id, the sum z1 plus z2 equals (a plus c) plus i times (b plus d).
- Addition properties: closure law (sum is a complex number), commutative law (z1 plus z2 equals z2 plus z1), associative law ((z1 plus z2) plus z3 equals z1 plus (z2 plus z3)), existence of additive identity (0 plus i0, denoted 0), existence of additive inverse (minus a plus i times minus b, denoted minus z).
- Difference: z1 minus z2 equals z1 plus (minus z2).
- Multiplication: For z1 equals a plus ib and z2 equals c plus id, the product z1 z2 equals (ac minus bd) plus i times (ad plus bc).
- Multiplication properties: closure law (product is a complex number), commutative law (z1 z2 equals z2 z1), associative law ((z1 z2) z3 equals z1 (z2 z3)), existence of multiplicative identity (1 plus i0, denoted 1), existence of multiplicative inverse for non-zero z equals a plus ib (a over (a squared plus b squared) plus i times (minus b over (a squared plus b squared)), denoted 1 over z or z to the power minus 1), distributive law (z1 (z2 plus z3) equals z1 z2 plus z1 z3 and (z1 plus z2) z3 equals z1 z3 plus z2 z3).
- Division: For z1 and z2 with z2 not equal to 0, the quotient z1 over z2 equals z1 times (1 over z2).
- Powers of i: i to the power 3 equals minus i, i to the power 4 equals 1, i to the power 5 equals i, i to the power 6 equals minus 1, and so on.
- Negative powers of i: i to the power minus 1 equals minus i, i to the power minus 2 equals minus 1, i to the power minus 3 equals i, i to the power minus 4 equals 1.
- General pattern for powers of i: For any integer k, i to the power 4k equals 1, i to the power (4k plus 1) equals i, i to the power (4k plus 2) equals minus 1, i to the power (4k plus 3) equals minus i.
- Square roots of negative real numbers: The square roots of minus 1 are i and minus i. The symbol square root of minus 1 means i only.
- The square roots of minus 3 are square root of 3 times i and minus square root of 3 times i. The symbol square root of minus 3 means square root of 3 times i only.
- In general, if a is a positive real number, square root of minus a equals square root of a times i.
- The rule square root of a times square root of b equals square root of (ab) holds when either a is greater than 0 and b is less than 0, or a is less than 0 and b is greater than 0. It does NOT hold if both a and b are negative real numbers.
- Identities for complex numbers: (z1 plus z2) squared equals z1 squared plus z2 squared plus 2 z1 z2. (z1 minus z2) squared equals z1 squared minus 2 z1 z2 plus z2 squared. (z1 plus z2) cubed equals z1 cubed plus 3 z1 squared z2 plus 3 z1 z2 squared plus z2 cubed. (z1 minus z2) cubed equals z1 cubed minus 3 z1 squared z2 plus 3 z1 z2 squared minus z2 cubed. z1 squared minus z2 squared equals (z1 plus z2)(z1 minus z2).

**Worked examples:**
- Example 2: Expressed (minus 5i) times (1 over 8 i) as 5 over 8 plus i0, and (minus i) times (2i) times (minus 1 over 8 i) cubed as 1 over 256 i, demonstrating simplification using powers of i.
- Example 3: Expanded (5 minus 3i) cubed to get minus 10 minus 198i, demonstrating the identity for (z1 minus z2) cubed.
- Example 4: Expressed (minus square root of 3 plus square root of minus 2) times (2 square root of 3 minus i) as (minus 6 plus square root of 2) plus square root of 3 times (1 plus 2 square root of 2) i, demonstrating multiplication with square roots of negative numbers.

**Callout cards:**
- title: "Addition and Multiplication" | content: "To add complex numbers, add real parts and imaginary parts separately. To multiply, use the rule (a+ib)(c+id) = (ac-bd) + i(ad+bc)."
- title: "Powers of i" | content: "For any integer k: i to the power 4k equals 1, i to the power (4k+1) equals i, i to the power (4k+2) equals minus 1, i to the power (4k+3) equals minus i."
- title: "Square Roots of Negative Numbers" | content: "For a positive real number a, the square root of minus a is square root of a times i. The rule square root of a times square root of b equals square root of (ab) does NOT hold when both a and b are negative."

### 4: The Modulus and the Conjugate of a Complex Number
eyebrow: Section 4.4

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- The modulus of a complex number z equals a plus ib, denoted by |z|, is the non-negative real number square root of (a squared plus b squared).
- The conjugate of a complex number z equals a plus ib, denoted by z bar, is the complex number a minus ib.
- The multiplicative inverse of a non-zero complex number z is given by z bar divided by |z| squared.
- Properties of modulus and conjugate for any two complex numbers z1 and z2: (i) |z1 z2| equals |z1| |z2|. (ii) |z1 / z2| equals |z1| / |z2| provided z2 is not 0. (iii) conjugate of (z1 z2) equals (conjugate of z1) times (conjugate of z2). (iv) conjugate of (z1 plus or minus z2) equals (conjugate of z1) plus or minus (conjugate of z2). (v) conjugate of (z1 / z2) equals (conjugate of z1) / (conjugate of z2) provided z2 is not 0.

**Worked examples:**
- Example 5: Found the multiplicative inverse of 2 minus 3i to be 2 over 13 plus 3 over 13 i, demonstrating the formula z inverse equals z bar over |z| squared.
- Example 6: Expressed (5 plus square root of 2 i) over (1 minus square root of 2 i) as 1 plus 2 square root of 2 i, and i to the power minus 35 as i, demonstrating division and simplification using powers of i.

**Callout cards:**
- title: "Modulus and Conjugate" | content: "The modulus |z| of a plus ib is the distance from the origin: square root of (a squared plus b squared). The conjugate z bar is a minus ib."
- title: "Multiplicative Inverse" | content: "The multiplicative inverse of a non-zero complex number z is z bar divided by |z| squared."

**Widget idea:** An interactive tool where users input a complex number and see its modulus, conjugate, and multiplicative inverse displayed.

**Key terms:** modulus, conjugate, |z|, z bar

### 5: Argand Plane and Polar Representation
eyebrow: Section 4.5

**Concepts (list every distinct rule, definition, and pattern — be exhaustive):**
- A complex number x plus iy corresponds to the ordered pair (x, y) and can be represented geometrically as the point P(x, y) in the XY-plane.
- The plane with a complex number assigned to each point is called the complex plane or the Argand plane.
- In the Argand plane, the modulus of x plus iy is the distance between the point P(x, y) and the origin O(0, 0).
- Points on the x-axis correspond to complex numbers of the form a plus i0 (real numbers). The x-axis is called the real axis.
- Points on the y-axis correspond to complex numbers of the form 0 plus ib (purely imaginary numbers). The y-axis is called the imaginary axis.
- The representation of a complex number z equals x plus iy and its conjugate z bar equals x minus iy in the Argand plane are the points P(x, y) and Q(x, minus y), respectively.
- Geometrically, the point (x, minus y) is the mirror image of the point (x, y) on the real axis.

**Worked examples:**
- None.

**Callout cards:**
- title: "The Argand Plane" | content: "Every complex number x plus iy corresponds to a unique point (x, y) in the plane. The x-axis is the real axis, and the y-axis is the imaginary axis."
- title: "Geometric Meaning of Modulus and Conjugate" | content: "The modulus |z| is the distance from the origin. The conjugate z bar is the mirror image of z across the real axis."

**Widget idea:** An interactive Argand plane where users can plot complex numbers and see their modulus, conjugate, and mirror image.

**Key terms:** Argand plane, complex plane, real axis, imaginary axis
